ENC 1101 introduces students to rhetorical concepts and audience-centered approaches to writing, including composing processes, language conventions and style, and critical analysis and engagement with written texts and other forms of communication. The NC grading policy applies to this course.

ENC 1101 emphasizes how writing works within and across communities, genres, processes, and languages to assist students in responding to today's writing scenes. Students will name and connect writing-related knowledge with writing-related practices to better understand, strengthen, extend, and transform their diverse literacy and language practices for future use. The reading and writing tasks in this course — analyses of writing processes and goal setting, identifying and producing genres responsive to rhetorical and linguistic variation, and gaining control over revision strategies — provide frameworks for effective and deliberate approaches to composing at UCF and beyond.

ENC 1101 includes an ePortfolio where students demonstrate the course learning outcomes. ePortfolios are also used as a direct measure for programmatic assessment.

Student Learning Outcomes

After successful completion of this course, students will be able to apply rhetorical knowledge to communicate for a range of audiences and purposes, employ critical thinking to analyze forms of communication, and engage in writing processes that involve drafting, revising, and reflecting.

ENC 1101-Specific Outcomes

  • Writing Processes & Adaptation. Describe and reflect on writing processes in order to flexibly adapt them to support your goals.
  • Multiple Literacies & Goal Setting. Demonstrate how you marshal/leverage your multiple literacies (speaking, listening, reading, multilingual writing, translating, multimodality, etc.) to support your writing processes.
  • Variation across Contexts. Identify, analyze, and reflect on variation in rhetorical and linguistic patterns, including your own, from a range of contexts (cultural, digital, workplace, and/or academic).
  • Decision Making & Production. Produce writing that demonstrates your ability to navigate choices and constraints when writing for specific audiences, genres, and purposes.
  • Writing and Power. Critically examine and act on the relationship among identity, literacy, language, and power.
  • Revision. Negotiate differences in and act with intention on feedback from readers when drafting, revising, and editing your writing.

Major Assignments

Major Assignment 1 · 10% / 100 pts · Due Sun, Sep 20, 2026 @ 11:59pm

Multimodal Literacy Narrative

Tell a story about a significant moment of your literacy (reading, writing, speaking) development and analyze your experience using at least one framework from our readings. Make a specific claim about how your past experiences with literacy and language have impacted your present writing identity.

Major Assignment 2 · 15% / 150 pts · Due Sun, Oct 25, 2026 @ 11:59pm

Textual Analysis of a Literacy Practice

Look closely at cultural texts and practices from your life — video games, social media, cooking, gardening, and so on — and use a framework from our readings to examine how those practices are shaped and constrained by social and cultural factors.

Major Assignment 3 · 15% / 150 pts · Due Sun, Nov 29, 2026 @ 11:59pm

Revise and Remediate

Critically examine and respond to feedback received on Major Assignment 1 or 2, then revise and remediate that content into a new multimodal version. Includes two deliverables: a reflective letter and the remediation itself.

Major Assignment 4 · 20% / 200 pts · Due Wed, Dec 9, 2026, 7:00–9:50 AM (Final Exam Period)

ePortfolio & Presentation

A purposeful digital collection curating your major writing assignments and a metacognitive reflection on your learning. Includes a 3–5 minute presentation on 1–2 pages of your ePortfolio during finals.

Drafting & Peer Review

No piece of writing simply appears finished. For Major Assignments 1, 2, and 3, you will compose at least three drafts and earn credit for each: a draft for peer review, a draft for instructor feedback, and a final graded draft. You will conduct Peer Review for every Major Assignment — your instructor will pair you with classmates and provide direction for feedback that benefits everyone involved.

Course Policies

Attendance: You are expected to attend every class. Four absences are allowed — your final grade drops one letter grade for every absence after that.
Late Work: Message the instructor 48 hours before a deadline for an alternative due date. Otherwise, a 5% penalty applies per day late, up to 10 days, after which the assignment receives a 0%.
Communication: All course communication goes through your @ucf.edu email or Webcourses. Expect a response within 24–48 hours on weekdays.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Use Policy

In this First-Year Composition course, writing is treated as a human, social, and reflective process. Your writing should represent your thinking, experiences, and voice. GenAI tools (such as ChatGPT) may be used in limited and purposeful ways only when explicitly allowed for a specific writing activity or assignment — they cannot replace the work of thinking, drafting, revising, or reflecting, and should not be used to produce final drafts or complete writing tasks for you.

When GenAI use is permitted, you will be required to write an "AI Use Statement" disclosing how and why you used the tool. If you are ever unsure whether or how GenAI can be used for a specific activity, ask before using it.

Academic Integrity

Academic misconduct includes plagiarism, unauthorized assistance, unauthorized communication during assessments, falsifying academic work, and soliciting or providing answers without authorization. Penalties range from a zero on the assignment to an "F" in the course, and may include a report to the Office of Student Conduct and Academic Integrity.

Take the zero, not a shortcut. No grade is worth compromising your integrity.
