THE COURSE
WHAT IS ENC 1102?
ENC 1102 emphasizes the discipline and community-specific nature of academic and public research writing through sustained, semester-long inquiry into topics related to writing, language, literacy, and rhetoric. Students learn to ask research questions that matter to specific communities, collect and analyze primary and secondary materials, and express their findings across multiple genres and modalities.
COURSE LEARNING OUTCOMES
►Generating Inquiry — generating and exploring genuine lines of inquiry related to writing, language, literacy, and rhetoric
►Multiple Ways of Writing — purposefully integrating multimodality, multiple languages, and multiliteracies
►Information Literacy — evaluating and acting on criteria for relevance, credibility, and ethics in research
►Research Genre Production — producing writing that navigates choices and constraints in academic and public genres
►Contributing Knowledge — drawing conclusions from primary evidence and placing that work in conversation with sources
►Revision — negotiating differences in and acting with intention on feedback from readers

LVL 5 · COURSE POLICIES
USE OF GENERATIVE AI
✅ APPROPRIATE USE
AI tools may be used to support brainstorming, outlining, and early-stage idea exploration. We should engage with these tools critically and intentionally, examining their limitations, biases, and ethical considerations. Specific guidelines will be communicated for each assignment.
🚫 NOT ACCEPTABLE
Using generative AI to complete peer reviews, rough drafts, or final drafts is a violation of academic integrity. The work you submit must represent your own thinking and your own writing. When in doubt about a specific use, contact the instructor before submitting your work.

LVL 6 · WEEKLY ACTIVITIES
READING RESPONSES
WHAT ARE THEY?
Reading responses are short, low-stakes written reflections on the week's assigned reading. They are not summaries — they are an opportunity to engage with the ideas, raise questions, make connections, or push back on something you found challenging or unconvincing.
📋 HOW THEY WORK
Each response is submitted through Canvas in response to a short prompt. Responses should demonstrate that you have read and engaged with the assigned material thoughtfully.
📅 WHEN & HOW MUCH
Due each Sunday by 11:59 PM. Worth 2 points each. 10 responses across the semester for a total of 20 points.
LVL 6 · WEEKLY ACTIVITIES
DISCUSSIONS
WHAT ARE THEY?
Discussions are the primary space for academic conversation in this course. Each week, a prompt asks you to engage with the assigned reading, your own research, or a broader question related to writing and rhetoric. Both your initial post and your replies should demonstrate genuine, substantive engagement — not surface-level responses.
📋 WHAT'S EXPECTED
Initial posts must engage meaningfully with the prompt. Replies should extend the conversation — ask a follow-up question, offer a different perspective, or build on a classmate's idea. Simply agreeing does not meet the expectation.
📅 WHEN & HOW MUCH
Initial post due Tuesday by 11:59 PM. Replies to at least two classmates due Friday by 11:59 PM. Worth 2 points per week, 12 discussions total — 24 points.
LVL 6 · WEEKLY ACTIVITIES
PEER REVIEW
WHAT IS IT?
Peer review is a required component of the major assignment process. For four assignments across the semester, you will be assigned two classmates whose drafts you are responsible for reading and providing written feedback on. Peer review is not proofreading — it is a substantive engagement with your classmate's argument, structure, evidence, and ideas.
📋 HOW IT WORKS
Drafts are posted to the Canvas Discussion Board by Tuesday. Assigned classmates complete and submit their reviews by Friday. A review guide with specific instructions is provided for each assignment.
📅 WHEN & HOW MUCH
Peer reviews are due Friday by 11:59 PM during major assignment weeks. Worth 4 points each. Four reviews across the semester for a total of 16 points.
